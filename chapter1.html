CLASS 12 · BIOLOGY · CHAPTER 01

Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

Introductory revision notes and practice questions.

← Back to all Class 12 Biology chapters
These are introductory revision notes, not a complete replacement for the prescribed NCERT textbook. Revise the full current CBSE syllabus as well.

1. Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

Sexual reproduction involves the formation and fusion of male and female gametes to produce a zygote. In flowering plants, the flower contains the reproductive structures.

Main reproductive parts

  • Androecium: The male reproductive whorl, made up of stamens.
  • Gynoecium: The female reproductive whorl, made up of one or more carpels.
  • Anther: Produces pollen grains.
  • Ovary: Contains ovules.

2. Formation of Male and Female Gametophytes

Male gametophyte

The anther contains pollen sacs in which microspore mother cells undergo meiosis to form microspores. These develop into pollen grains.

Female gametophyte

The ovule contains a megaspore mother cell. It undergoes meiosis to produce four megaspores. Generally, three degenerate and the functional megaspore develops into the embryo sac.

A typical mature angiosperm embryo sac is seven-celled and eight-nucleate.

3. Pollination

Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma.

  • Autogamy: Pollen is transferred to the stigma of the same flower.
  • Geitonogamy: Pollen is transferred between different flowers of the same plant.
  • Xenogamy: Pollen is transferred to a flower on a different plant of the same species.

Agents of pollination

Wind, water and animals such as insects and birds can act as pollinating agents.

4. Pollen-Pistil Interaction

Pollen-pistil interaction includes the events between the arrival of pollen on the stigma and the entry of the pollen tube into the ovule.

The pistil can recognise compatible pollen. Compatible pollen may germinate and develop a pollen tube that carries the male gametes towards the embryo sac.

5. Double Fertilisation

Double fertilisation is a characteristic feature of angiosperms. It involves two fusion events.

  • Syngamy: One male gamete fuses with the egg to form a diploid zygote.
  • Triple fusion: The other male gamete fuses with the two polar nuclei, forming the primary endosperm nucleus, usually triploid.

These two events together are called double fertilisation.

6. After Fertilisation

  • The zygote develops into an embryo.
  • The endosperm provides nourishment to the developing embryo.
  • The ovule generally develops into a seed.
  • The ovary generally develops into a fruit.

7. Special Modes of Reproduction

Apomixis: Formation of seeds without fertilisation.

Parthenocarpy: Development of a fruit without fertilisation; it often results in seedless fruits.

Polyembryony: Formation of more than one embryo in a seed.

Quick Revision

Anther → Pollen grains

Ovule → Embryo sac

Zygote → Embryo

Ovule → Seed

Ovary → Fruit

Important Practice Questions

1. What is pollination?

Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of a flower.

2. Define double fertilisation.

It is the occurrence of syngamy and triple fusion in the embryo sac of an angiosperm.

3. What is a typical embryo sac?

A typical mature angiosperm embryo sac is seven-celled and eight-nucleate.

4. Differentiate between apomixis and parthenocarpy.

Apomixis involves seed formation without fertilisation. Parthenocarpy is fruit development without fertilisation.

5. What develops from the ovule and ovary?

The ovule generally develops into a seed, while the ovary generally develops into a fruit.
